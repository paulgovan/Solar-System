About this orrery

Planet positions are computed in your browser from JPL’s Keplerian orbital elements (E. M. Standish), solving Kepler’s equation for each planet. They agree with NASA JPL Horizons to within a few arcminutes between 1800 and 2050, and stay within a fraction of a degree from 3000 BC to 3000 AD.

Planet spin axes and rotation follow the IAU WGCCRE models, so the day/night side and Saturn’s ring tilt are correct for the date shown. The Moon follows Meeus’ truncated ELP-2000/82 lunar theory, and eclipses are computed from the true Sun–Earth–Moon geometry. The Milky Way is placed in its true position on the sky.

Compressed scale keeps every direction exact but shrinks distances (square root) and enlarges planets so you can see them. True scale uses real distances and sizes — the planets become specks, which is what space is really like.

With Imagery on (I), Mercury, Earth, the Moon, Mars, Jupiter and Pluto show real global maps, loaded when you visit them: NASA Earth Observatory Blue Marble NG (day), Black Marble 2016 (night lights) and Blue Marble clouds; LRO WAC colour and LOLA relief (NASA SVS CGI Moon Kit); MESSENGER MDIS, Viking colourised MDIM and New Horizons global mosaics (USGS Astrogeology); Cassini PIA07782 (NASA/JPL/University of Arizona). All public domain. Venus, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune, and every body with Imagery off, are procedural and artistic.
