About this orrery

Planet positions are computed in your browser from JPL’s Keplerian orbital elements (E. M. Standish), solving Kepler’s equation for each planet. They agree with NASA JPL Horizons to within a few arcminutes between 1800 and 2050, and stay within a fraction of a degree from 3000 BC to 3000 AD.

Planet spin axes and rotation follow the IAU WGCCRE models, so the day/night side and Saturn’s ring tilt are correct for the date shown. The Moon uses a low-precision lunar theory. The Milky Way is placed in its true position on the sky.

Compressed scale keeps every direction exact but shrinks distances (square root) and enlarges planets so you can see them. True scale uses real distances and sizes — the planets become specks, which is what space is really like.

Earth’s coastlines come from Natural Earth map data; its colours, clouds and city lights are approximations. Other surfaces are generated procedurally and are artistic, not maps.
